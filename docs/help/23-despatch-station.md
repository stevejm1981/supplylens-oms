# Despatch Station: the warehouse app

> `/despatch-station`

A packing-bench view of the same despatch documents the rest of the OMS uses: pick with barcode scans, pack with weights, generate a carrier label, confirm. It owns **zero business rules**: every button drives the standard despatch flow, so stock, ledger, COGS and the accounting journal all happen exactly as if the office had done it. The DPD label is mocked; the production build swaps that one step for DPD's shipping API on the customer's own business account. Batch-tracked lines show an amber **Take lot** note (FEFO, earliest best-before first), and confirming really consumes those lots on the movement ledger.

## The demo, step by step (5 minutes)

1. **Batch pick option.** Tick several queued orders and `Print job list` prints ONE consolidated sheet: a grab list totalling every physical SKU across the wave (bundles exploded to components, pack lines kept as their own grab lines with outer barcodes, each line showing which orders need it), plus a "sort into orders" table for the bench. One walk instead of five. Read-only: despatches are still created per order at the station.
2. **Set the scene on the queue.** Open `Despatch Station` in the Warehouse group. Talk through what the packer sees: orders ready to pick, oldest required-date first, each showing its lines, carrier service and badges: a *Picking in progress* order can be **resumed** (SO-0012 in the seed), and pre-orders are flagged. In production this queue fills itself from the customer's order system via SupplyLens: nobody keys anything.
3. **Start SO-0017** (The Range, via Mirakl: the retailer pack order). Click `Start picking`. A despatch document is created behind the scenes; the screen shows both lines with 0 / target progress.
4. **Print the pick list.** Click `Print pick list`: a clean paper document opens (no app chrome) with tick-boxes, big pick quantities, units spelled out (16 × PACK6 = 96 ea), barcodes, shipping instructions and a picked-by signature line. **Virtual bundles explode into indented component rows**: order 10 BBQ bundles and the sheet says pick 10 fire pits, 10 tongs, 20 charcoal, each with its own tick-box and barcode. This is what walks the shelves; the screen at the bench is what *verifies*. Hand the printout across the table: paper is persuasive. Batch-tracked lines (the seeded drinks) carry their FEFO suggestion right on the sheet: "Take lot ELD-2547 (BBE…) ×120", oldest best-before first, spilling into the next lot when one runs dry.
5. **Scan the outer barcode.** Click into the scan box, type `5060871330288` and press Enter: that's the pack-of-6 outer GTIN on the tongs cases. The line flashes *✓ GRD-TONGS-01: 1 × PACK6 (6 ea)* and ticks up. Repeat a few times (or use the + button to fill to 16: say "this is what the scanner does"). Then scan `5060871330295` for the charcoal cases (5 of them). Point out the line goes green when complete.
6. **Show mis-pick prevention.** Scan `5060871330011` (the fire pit: not on this order): the station refuses it in red. Scan a completed line again: *line already complete*. This is the "wrong item in the box" problem solved at source.
7. **Short-pick escape hatch.** Mention (don't do): if only 14 packs are on the shelf, the packer proceeds with `Short-pick 14 of 21`: the order stays partially fulfilled and the balance re-queues. No stuck orders.
8. **Pack it.** Enter the expected **Carriage cost** too if you know it (what DPD will charge you, not the customer): it accrues as cost to serve on confirmation. The weight pre-fills from the catalogue (16 packs of tongs + 5 cases of 5 kg charcoal: a real number, from real product weights); override with the scale reading. Parcels and service are editable; the order's service pre-fills.
9. **Generate the DPD label.** A label renders with the delivery address (auto-snapshotted from the customer's named location), order reference, weight, parcel count and a tracking number. Be upfront: *this label is mocked: the real one is a single API call to DPD on your account, printed to your thermal printer.* That honesty lands better than pretending.
10. **Confirm despatch: then show the magic.** Click `Confirm despatch` and open the order in Sales Orders: fulfilment FULFILLED, tracking on the despatch, COGS and margin populated. Then **Movements**: −96 eaches of tongs, −20 of charcoal, in the ledger with running balances. Then say: "and the accounting entry for what that stock cost just queued itself for Xero" (`GET /api/v1/stock-journals?status=PENDING` if the audience is technical). One scan-and-click at the bench moved stock, costed the sale, and prepped the books.
11. **Close the loop.** The confirmation the station just fired is the same message a 3PL or any WMS can send via the API: so the same workflow runs whether the warehouse is in-house (this app) or outsourced (the 3PL's WMS). And for a customer on their previous inventory platform, SupplyLens routes the identical confirmation into *their* system instead: the app works with what they already run.

> **Tip:** Barcodes to have on a sticky note for the demo Tongs pack-of-6 outer: `5060871330288` · Charcoal case-of-4 outer: `5060871330295` · Fire pit (for the mis-pick moment): `5060871330011`. Typing + Enter is exactly what a USB scanner sends.

> **Careful:** Reset between run-throughs Confirming a despatch really despatches it, that's the point. To demo twice, re-run `npx prisma db seed` for a fresh queue, or pick a different order the second time.
